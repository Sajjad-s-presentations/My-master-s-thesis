\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{چکیدۀ انگلیسی}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
\begingroup
\setstretch{1.15}
Text-based analysis of suicide-related signals requires both valid evaluation and
careful treatment of sensitive mental-health information. This thesis examines
text classification, distributed probability aggregation, and authenticated
homomorphic weighted aggregation. The English Suicide Watch dataset contains
232,074 raw records. Deduplication, stratified splitting, and partition-aware
cleaning yield 148,380 training, 37,086 validation, and 46,342 test records.
A 100-dimensional Word2Vec representation is learned inside each training fold.
Random Forest, Decision Tree, XGBoost, and Gaussian\lr{--}Bernoulli soft voting are
compared on validation data. The model and threshold are frozen before final test
evaluation. The centralized evaluation selects \BestModelEnglish, with threshold
\BestThreshold, test accuracy \BestTestAccuracyPercent\ percent, and positive-class
F1 of \BestTestFone.

The distributed experiment assigns every training record exactly once using
class-stratified, approximately IID partitions for 10, 20, 50, and 100 clients.
Each client learns its own Word2Vec and XGBoost model. Predictions are combined by
a sample-count-weighted mean, rather than by averaging independently learned
tree parameters or embedding coordinates. Archived test accuracies at
validation-selected thresholds are 91.58, 90.93, 89.30, and 87.00 percent,
respectively. The 10-client scenario is selected using validation F1. Only one
seed is available per scenario; across-run uncertainty is not estimated.

The SBHE-Benaloh implementation verifies an Ed25519 signature over a deterministic
transcript containing identity, round, nonce, ciphertext, and weight before
aggregation. The signature is kept outside the ciphertext algebra. Correctness
requires an explicit fixed-point representation and sufficient plaintext-space
capacity. Archived 256-bit research-key benchmarks report mean total times of
approximately 119.02 and 275.68 milliseconds for 100 and 300 clients. These are
separate cryptographic benchmarks, not end-to-end encrypted classification
measurements. The analysis explicitly distinguishes the private-key-owning
server from external observers and documents the absence of range proofs and
threshold decryption. The findings support further research and do not establish
clinical readiness or production cryptographic security.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} Natural language processing; distributed learning;
stratified partitioning; Word2Vec; threshold selection; homomorphic encryption;
Benaloh; digital signatures; privacy.
